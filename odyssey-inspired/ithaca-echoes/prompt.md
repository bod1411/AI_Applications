## Research Notes

Source track: "Ithaca" — score by Ludwig Göransson for Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey* (2026).

Key findings that shaped this prompt:
- Nolan directed Göransson to avoid a traditional orchestra, pushing instead toward "ancient instruments" — aulos (Greek double-reed flute) and lyre — layered with 35 bronze gongs and synth.
- Structure arc: pastoral/intimate opening → sustained tranquil ambience → low synth surges introduce tension → percussive, war-like gong/drum climax that restates the main motif.
- Reported tempo ramp roughly 100 → 173 BPM across the track (slow reflective build to driven climax) — treated as approximate, not composer-confirmed.
- Emotional register: melancholic, weary homecoming longing rather than triumphant fanfare — the piece equates "Ithaca" (place) with "Penelope" (wife), both representing "home" in Odysseus's psychology.
- No confirmed key/chord data exists publicly for this brand-new score; a minor/modal (Dorian/Phrygian-leaning) palette was assumed stylistically rather than researched fact.
